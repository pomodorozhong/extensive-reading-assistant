# Features

A detailed reference for how the app behaves. For a quick overview, see the [README](../README.md).


## Reading and word lookup

A small web app for extensive reading: set a CEFR level, generate a story at that level, and tap a word when you need a definition. A compact note appears beneath the selected occurrence’s complete line, keeping the words on that line in place. Definitions use [Princeton WordNet](https://wordnet.princeton.edu/) bundled as static files (not generated by the LLM). Tap the word again or choose **Close** to dismiss the note and keep reading. Looked-up words stay highlighted as unknown; choose **Mark as known** in the note or the story’s **Unknown words** list after learning a word. Familiar words need no annotation.

Stories, word marks, and settings are stored in this browser (`localStorage`). There is no account or server.

## Vocabulary

Open **Vocabulary** from the desktop/mobile menu to review unknown words across stories. Words are grouped without regard to case, with each source story's saved definition, sentence, and link to the occurrence. Missing definitions remain visible. The collection has its own browser-local storage: removing a saved story keeps its unknown words, definitions, and sentences, with the source labeled **Story removed**. **Mark as known** in Vocabulary clears a word from the collection and marks it known in all remaining saved stories and enabled debugging fixtures. Marking a word known inside one story clears only that source; another unknown source, including a removed saved story, keeps the word tracked.

Existing saved marks are imported automatically when the app opens; subsequent story and word updates keep the collection current. No re-marking is needed. With **Show CEFR fixture articles in My Stories** enabled, unknown fixture words appear too, with a **Debug fixture** label and source link. Turning the option off hides fixture sources and words known only from fixtures; words with an unknown saved-story source, including a removed saved story, remain. Turning it back on restores fixture words still marked unknown. Fixture marks and definitions stay in their existing separate storage, so hiding fixtures does not archive them as removed saved stories. This collection tracks words still being learned, with no flashcards or review scheduling. Clearing browser data also clears Vocabulary.

Vocabulary groups unknown words into approximate A1–C2 sections, followed by Unlisted, using the bundled offline CEFR data independently of story and Settings levels. Words are alphabetized within each nonempty section, and each section shows its word count. Each distinct saved definition appears once, followed by the sentences and story links for every source using it. Different definitions remain separate, and sources without a definition have their own unavailable group.

See [Vocabulary verification steps](vocabulary-verification.md) for a disposable fixture and removal/persistence checks.

## Approximate vocabulary difficulty

Enable **Settings → Debugging → Show approximate vocabulary difficulty** to display a report against each story's recorded CEFR level. This option is off by default. Validation runs locally with bundled A1–C2 vocabulary data, needs no API key, and works offline after the app is cached. Above-level words include occurrence counts and a percentage; unlisted words and detected names appear separately. Reports are advisory and do not block reading or saving.

See [dataset attribution, calculation, limitations, and verification](../data/cefr/README.md). The bundled dataset has its own terms, including CC BY-SA 4.0 for the derived vocabulary JSON.

For a detailed implementation walkthrough, flowcharts, and links to related code, see [How CEFR vocabulary validation works](cefr-vocabulary-validation.md).

To try the report without generating a story, also enable **Settings → Debugging → Show CEFR fixture articles in My Stories**. This option is off by default and adds six bundled articles labeled A1–C2. They are illustrative debugging samples, not certified CEFR texts. Their word marks and definitions are stored separately; switching the option off hides the fixtures and preserves your saved stories.

## Introduction

On first use, a six-step introduction explains extensive reading and the app's workflow: pick a level and generate a story, read for the story, tap an unfamiliar word for its meaning, review words in Vocabulary, and where the Gemini key goes, that generation needs internet, and that data stays in this browser. Each step has a short CSS animation; with the system's reduced-motion setting on, every step shows its finished frame instead. Back, Next, and Skip are always available, the arrow keys also change steps, and the last step offers **Choose a level and start** (New Story) and **Check my level** (the level check). Viewing it needs no key.

It opens automatically only when no outcome is recorded and no stories are saved, so existing readers are not interrupted. Finishing or skipping (including Esc) saves `completed` or `skipped` in the `era.v1.onboarding` localStorage entry and changes nothing else. **Settings → Introduction → Show the introduction again** reopens it. `npm run test:onboarding` covers the stored outcome and the first-use rule.

## Choose a story

New Story offers an optional topic, topic examples, a text type, a CEFR vocabulary level, a reading length, a supported model selector, and collapsed **Style options**. An example fills the topic without submitting; the examples follow the chosen text type, and with none chosen they include story genres such as a mystery or a ghost story. The text type is picked in two steps: a mode (**Stories**, **Real life**, **Ideas**, or **Practical**), then one of its 16 forms, such as a fable, a diary entry, an opinion essay, or a letter. Picking a mode selects its first form, and **Any** leaves the text type to the model, which usually writes a story. Each form changes the prompt's request (for example, "a complete set of diary entries") and adds a short guide and its own dialogue rule; a play script is written as lines with speaker names. Text types are bound to the vocabulary level: each has a level range in `src/lib/generation-options.ts`, every mode offers at least one form at every level, and the help line names forms that a higher level unlocks. Fairy tales and fables stop at B2, and reflective and opinion essays start at B1. Changing the level clears a selected form the new level doesn't offer and says so; **Use *level* for New Story** on the level check does the same. Style options hold a tone (cozy, tense, funny, or thoughtful) and a point of view (first or third person), each defaulting to **Any**, and **Varied sentence length**, on by default, which asks for a mix of short and long sentences and paragraphs so the text has rhythm; the collapsed row lists the choices that differ from the defaults. Forms with their own point of view, such as a diary (first person), a news report (third person), or instructions (no narrator), turn the point-of-view control off and ignore the stored choice. At A1 and A2, stories ask for no made-up words so every word can be looked up; explanations and biographies ask for accurate facts and explain topic words. Notes under the form say when these rules apply. The default level is A2 and the initial reading length is 10 minutes. A saved Settings level takes precedence; choices on New Story apply to that story and do not overwrite Settings. The model list currently offers **Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite**; its display label and request identifier are maintained together in `src/lib/generation-options.ts`. A supported saved model is selected initially; other saved model values fall back to the supported default in this form.

An "i" button after **Vocabulary level**, on New Story and in Settings, opens a dialog that describes each level A1–C2 in plain language, suggests choosing the level where reading feels easy, and links to the in-app reading level check.

## Reading level check

The reading level check (`/level-check`) shows three short sets of words, and the reader ticks every word they know; unticked words count as unknown. The first set samples every band A1–C2 of the bundled CEFR data with two words each, plus four made-up words. Each later set is tuned by all answers so far. It probes the two bands where the reader's level seems to change, meaning the current suggestion and the band above it (A1–A2 or C1–C2 at the ends), with four unseen words each plus four more made-up words. Each band's share of known words is corrected for guessing as (hits − made-up tick rate) / (1 − made-up tick rate). The suggested level is the highest band reached at 80% or more without a lower band falling short, or A1 if A1 itself falls short. Ticking more than a quarter of the made-up words shows a warning. **Use *level* for New Story** changes only the New Story draft's level, keeping its topic, text type (when the level offers it), style, length, and model. An optional checkbox also saves the level as the Settings default.

The check runs entirely in the browser, needs no key or network, and keeps no answers or results. It measures recognition of single words only, not reading comprehension, and its thresholds are not calibrated against learner data, so the result is an estimate for picking reading material, not a certified CEFR result. The word lists live in `src/lib/level-check.ts`; `npm run test:level-check` confirms each real word's band and that no made-up word appears in the CEFR data or the bundled dictionary.

The form expands to 1,024 pixels on wide screens and keeps 16-pixel side gutters on phones. Vocabulary level, reading length, and model stack below 768 pixels. From 768 to 1,023 pixels, vocabulary level fills its own row above length and model. At 1,024 pixels and wider, all three share one row, with extra room for the six vocabulary levels.

Reading lengths use these prompt targets:

| Requested reading length | Approximate word target |
| --- | --- |
| 5 min | 400–600 words |
| 10 min | 800–1,200 words |
| 15 min | 1,200–1,800 words |

These are product targets rather than measured reading speeds. Reading time varies by learner, and generated length is advisory. New stories store the submitted level, optional topic, text type, tone, point of view (unless the text type sets it), whether varied sentence length was requested, and requested `readingMinutes`. They also keep a `generation` record of the request sent: the model, temperature, target word range, and the exact system and user prompts, never the API key or endpoint. Older stories without these fields remain readable, and a story or draft saved with the earlier `theme` field loads it as its topic. My Stories and Story View show a story's text type. Below the story, a **How this story was made** card lists the saved choices and, when recorded, the model, temperature, and word target, with the prompts collapsed; stories without these details and debug fixtures show no card. Credentials and API endpoint configuration stay in Settings. The New Story draft (topic, text type, tone, point of view, level, length, and model) is kept across visits to Settings and reloads.

## Settings and the Gemini API key

In **Settings**:

1. Choose a CEFR band (A1–C2, initially A2). New stories use this level.
2. Paste a [Gemini API key](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey) from Google AI Studio.
3. Optionally change the model (default `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`).

Without a key, **Generate Story** on New Story opens a dialog that explains how to get a key and how it is stored, with the same **Remember key on this device** choice. Saving it there works like saving it in Settings and then generates with your current choices; offline, it saves the key without generating.

By default the key is remembered in this browser's `localStorage`, in its own entry separate from other settings (keys saved by earlier versions move there automatically). Turn off **Remember key on this device** to keep it in memory only: reloading, updating the app, or closing the tab then forgets it. **Forget key** removes it from both places and keeps your other settings and stories. The key is sent only to Google's Gemini API at `https://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/v1beta` when you generate a story. Any other API base URL is refused before the key is attached, and redirects are treated as failures. Development builds (`npm run dev`) also accept loopback addresses such as `http://127.0.0.1:5175/v1beta` for the local fixture. Use a key dedicated to this app, restrict it to the Gemini API and, if you host the app publicly, to your site's HTTP referrer, and set a billing alert or spending limit in Google Cloud. These reduce misuse but are not guarantees; delete the key in Google AI Studio if it may have been exposed.

## Install and read offline

The published site can be installed as a web app from a supported mobile browser. On iPhone or iPad, open the site in Safari, tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**, keep **Open as Web App** enabled if prompted, then launch it from the new home-screen icon. On Android, use the browser menu’s **Install app** or **Add to Home screen** action.

Open the app online once and wait for the **Offline reading is ready on this device** message. The app shell, bundled dictionary, and stories saved in that same browser context can then be read offline on this device. Story generation still needs an internet connection because it calls Gemini.

Stories and settings stay in local storage for the browser context where they were created and do not sync between devices. On iOS, an installed Home Screen web app has separate storage from Safari, so stories saved in Safari are not copied into the installed app automatically. Use Safari to keep reading those stories, or save new stories from the installed app for offline use there.

When an update is available, choose **Reload** in the update message to load the latest version.
